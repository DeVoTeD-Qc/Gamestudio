New parts, shared by every direction

The pieces first

Every direction is built from these parts, so they can be judged on their own. All sizes here are the portrait sizes.

Hit burst: the damage icon (point 4)

One yellow burst with the weapon's hit number, on every gun, melee weapon and Codex entry. It replaces "P4". The damage you take is still the red chip on the zombie (strength minus hit).

Kill sockets: capacity at a glance (points 5, 6)

One socket per kill the gun can hold. Filled sockets show the zombie's art and number, the next socket shows what it accepts, empty ones are dashed. Sockets are 36 × 46 px in portrait, about twice the area of the v8 stack tiles.

Room price on the image (point 3)

A padlock plate hangs on the room art. Locked: dark plate, price, and a thin bar for how close you are. Affordable: the plate turns gold, the padlock opens and the tile glows. Open: green USE. Used: grey until the next wave. The separate price button is gone; tapping the gold plate opens the room.

Short tooltips (point 8)

Rule: one bold line says what it is and its numbers now; one plain line says what it does for you. Under about 70 characters a line. The full card stays one more tap away in the zoom.

Zombie strength: four options (point 7)

Same four zombies (2, 5, 8, 11) at the portrait street-card size, scaled to 60% so all options fit side by side. Each direction below uses a different one so you can see it in place. The tier (Shambler 1–3, Lurcher 4–6, Bruiser 7–9, Hulk 10–11) is the second channel: shape or pips, not just colour, so it reads for colour-blind players.

A

Board grid

closest to v8 · lowest risk

The top board becomes a grid of six equal tiles: wave stack, pile and boss on one row, the three rooms on the next. Every deck is the same size, so a wave weighs exactly as much as a room. The big wave number sits in the HUD at the far left.

  • Wave stack, pile, boss and rooms are all 105 × 98 px tiles in portrait; rooms are drawn as decks too (they hold 3 cards).
  • Wave numeral: a red chip in the HUD, always in the same corner.
  • Strength: number + skull pips. Melee slot shows Bare Hands as the default draft.
  • Landscape: waves, pile and boss on the left; rooms on the right; same tile size both sides.

B

Door row

recommended

The wave and the three rooms form one row of four equal "doors": everything that feeds the pile, side by side. The wave door carries the big wave number itself. The draw pile moves down next to the street, where cards are dealt from, so the flights in and out are short and easy to follow.

  • Wave door and room doors are 81 × 134 px in portrait; the street cards grow to 175 × 199 px (v8: 175 × 160).
  • Wave numeral: painted large on the wave door; the boss is named under it ("then BOSS 9").
  • Strength: threat badge. Melee slot shows a found Fire Axe with durability pips draft.
  • Landscape: doors become a left column, guns a right column, street and pile in the middle.

C

Corner count

boldest · most CoD-like

A huge red wave number owns the bottom-left corner, painted over the draw pile, with the waiting waves counted under it. Waves and pile become one object: the horde deck. Rooms run along the top as a strip of doors, with the boss at its end.

  • Wave numeral: 96 px tall in portrait, 120 px in landscape; it is the first thing you see.
  • One less object on screen (pile and waves merged), so the street cards get 175 × 220 px.
  • Guns become slim bars beside the corner; sockets shrink to 24 × 36 px. Strength: number + strength bar.
  • Risk: "which wave is waiting" is a line of text, not a stack you can see.

Recommendation: B, Door row, with the threat badge

B is the only direction where the head's first point is literally true: the wave and the rooms are the same object in the same row, and both visibly feed one pile that sits right where the cards are dealt. It gives the street cards about 20% more height than v8 and keeps full-size kill sockets. In landscape it gives guns their own column, which is where the bigger stacks need room. If the move of the pile feels too new, A is the safe fallback with the same parts. C's big corner numeral can be borrowed: B can paint its wave number larger if the head wants more CoD in it.
