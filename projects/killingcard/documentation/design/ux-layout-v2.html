Match screen: layout v2

Three directions for the match screen. Every direction fits a hand of up to 7 cards with no scrolling, shows the Operator mark, uses one icon per concept and keeps a strict text order: name, then cost and stats, then one rule line. Flavour text appears only in the inspector. Grey-blue panels stand in for the calling-card art window; this page shows no licensed art.

1. Why the hand scrolls today

These screenshots come from the current build with the shot tools (tests/tools/shot_hand.gd, plus a scratch variant that sets the hand to 4 and 7 cards). They use public art only. The hand's size comes from BoardMetrics.hand_fit().

Root cause: the board is sized first and the hand gets the leftovers. When those are too small, hand_fit() chooses scrolling over smaller faces. Three things also eat tray space: the Operator's big block, the killstreak row, and the card faces themselves, which only have one size. All three directions below turn this around. The hand gets a guaranteed area for 7 cards plus the Operator, the board takes what remains, and card faces switch between a full, a mid and a compact form so they never need to scroll.

2. Directions A / B / C

Hand Landscape phone Scale

Frames are drawn in CSS px at the device's real size (1 CSS px = 1 iOS point). On a screen narrower than a frame, “Fit” scales it down; “1:1” shows it at true size and lets the frame scroll inside its own box. Desktop is drawn at 1440×900 with UI scale 1.5. The amber line is the “what just happened / what now” line the studio rule asks for. Cards that cost 4 (Sniper, Riot Shield, Private IV) can't be paid with 3 Supply: their cost hex turns grey and the card sits 4 px lower, but the art keeps full colour.

A · Keep the board, fix the hand

Recommended

The board keeps 8 rows of 4:1 banners at full height. The tray changes. The three killstreak sockets move into the left strip, the Operator becomes the first card of the hand grid (marked by its brass frame, not by a block of its own), and UNDO + READY share one row. The hand has a fixed 2-column grid. From 4 to 5 cards, faces are mid (name, cost, art, one rule line, FP and Armor). From 6 to 7 they turn compact: the name plate sits on the art and the rule is one long-press away. Cards never overlap and never scroll. On narrow phones the board shrinks a little, never the hand.

B · Smaller board, bigger hand

The board banners shrink to 120×30 pt (about 73% of today's). That frees about 130 pt of width for the tray, so the hand shows full portrait faces at every count: 3 columns up to 5 cards plus the Operator, 4 columns at 6–7. Every card shows its name, cost, art, rule (up to 2 lines), trait, FP and Armor without a press. The space left under the board holds the event line.

C · Point man up front, hand along the bottom

A new arrangement. In each column only the point man (the front unit, the one that fights first) shows as a full 4:1 banner. The three behind it become slim rows: a small art thumbnail, the name, FP and Armor. That makes the board short enough for a hand rail along the bottom, like most card games, with every card side by side. The Operator, Supply, killstreaks, the event line and READY live in a right-hand column. On narrow screens the rail overlaps cards, and each fanned card carries its cost, FP and Armor down its left edge so the overlap never hides a number, and the name runs up the card's visible edge. In portrait, 5–7 cards take two rows instead of overlapping.

3. Card face, text hierarchy and the Operator mark

Text hierarchy (one rule, every card)

  1. Name: Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, upper case, the brightest text on the card (12–13 pt hand, 20 pt inspector).
  2. Cost and stats: figures on their own shapes. Amber hex for cost, a ring in your side's colour for FP, a steel shield for Armor. 13–14 pt, the same size as the name.
  3. One rule line: the keyword icon, then at most about 6 words (Barlow Medium 10.5–11 pt, never under 10). Full rules sit one long-press away.
  4. Flavour: inspector only, at the bottom, in italics.

Cut from the faces

  • The “UNIT” / “STANDARD ISSUE” type line: the frame already says the kind (steel unit, khaki tactic, brass Operator).
  • The “Issued, signed for, expected back in one piece” flavour on hand cards.
  • “Respawns at your next Deploy if it goes down.” becomes “Back next Deploy if down.”
  • “Resolves immediately: draw 2.” becomes the icon plus “Draw 2 now.”
  • “TAP A CARD TO RAISE IT” in the empty portrait well, the word “SUPPLY” next to the hex, and “SETS none”.

Three faces, one frame

The hand picks the largest face that fits, and never scrolls.

Operator mark

A 3 px brass frame (#C9A227) and a brass OPERATOR tab with the dog-tag icon on the top edge. Both are the same in the hand, on the board, in the deck builder and in the inspector. No other card uses brass, so the mark is readable without colour too: the tab carries the word and the icon. Elite reward cards keep their star but use the rarity keyline, not brass.

4. Tooltip before / after (long press or right click)

Riot Shield. “Before” is built from today's texts: the card line, the keyword entries and the glossary entries in card_info.gd, plus the kind of placeholder line the art audit found in the inspector. “After” keeps only the lines that matter for the decision.

Before · 9 lines, about 80 words

Riot Shield

UNIT · VANGUARD · Cost 4 · FP 1 · Armor 3

Suppress 1. Cover 1.

Suppress 1: At the start of the firefight, the enemy point man here gets -1 Firepower this round. Several Suppress add up.

Cover 1: Each hit on this unit deals 1 less damage (minimum 0).

Firepower (FP): Damage this unit deals when it shoots: the number in the reticle ring, bottom-right (teal ring yours, red the rival's; a green number is buffed)…

Armor: Health: the number on the grey ballistic plate at the bottom-right corner (both sides)…

Banner: Riot Shield (placeholder)

After · 4 lines, about 25 words

Riot Shield

413 VANGUARD
Suppress 1: rival point man here: −1 this round.
Cover 1: each hit on it deals 1 less.
Vanguard 1/2 in play: set bonus at 2.
What FP and Armor mean lives in the Codex glossary, not on every card. Flavour text, when a card has some, goes here in italics.

Rules for every tooltip: the first line is the name with cost and stats as icons. Then one line per keyword, as icon, keyword, what it does now (with the real numbers). Then the trait and its set count. No glossary text, no placeholder lines, no “minimum 0” style edge cases (those live in the Codex).

5. Icon consistency: one concept, one icon, everywhere

Recommendation: A. It fixes the head's complaint everywhere (no scroll at 4, or at 7, on any phone or desktop) and keeps the board's calling cards at today's size, which is the point of the pitch build. It is also the smallest change. Take two parts from the others: B's full face whenever there is room (A already uses mid and full faces at 4 cards on desktop and portrait), and C's rule that a fan carries its numbers on the left edge, kept in reserve if a future screen ever needs overlap. Build order: (1) size the tray first in BoardMetrics, with killstreaks in the strip and the Operator in the grid; (2) three face forms in the hand card; (3) the brass Operator frame and tab (already in progress with the Technical Artist); (4) the icon pass from the table above; (5) tooltip cuts.

7. Questions for the head

  1. Which layout? (a) Recommended: A, keep the board and fix the hand. (b) B, a smaller board with the biggest hand. (c) C, point men up front with a hand rail along the bottom.
  2. At 6–7 cards, what gives? (a) Recommended: cards switch to a compact face (name, cost, art, FP, Armor), with the rule line one long-press away. (b) Cards keep the full face and overlap like a fan; tap one to raise it.
  3. Where does the Operator sit? (a) Recommended: first card of the hand grid, the same size as the others, told apart by the brass frame and OPERATOR tab. (b) Its own larger block beside READY, as today.
  4. Killstreak sockets? (a) Recommended: in the left strip under the streak counter (icon plus “2/3”). (b) At the top of the tray, as today (this costs the hand a row on narrow phones).
  5. Rival control mark. Today it is a red diamond, the same shape as Field. (a) Recommended: the column letter on a solid teal plate (yours) or a hatched red plate (rival), so the diamond means only Field. (b) Keep the diamond.
