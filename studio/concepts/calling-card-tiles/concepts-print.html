The basics

The shapes

Calling card = banner

A 1×4 tile: four cells in a line. It lies across or stands up. It has 2 long sides and 2 short ends.

Emblem = square badge

A 2×2 tile. Same area as a banner (4 cells). It has 8 border cells, 2 per side, so it makes a good hub.

The problem: banners alone leave dead cells

A dead cell is an empty cell that no remaining piece can ever cover. Long thin pieces jam: the gaps they leave are mostly 1 cell wide.

21%of an 8×8 board is dead after random play (13.7 of 64 cells)
99.7%of random games strand at least one cell
5.6%still dead with careful (“tidy”) play
Nevera perfect fill on 7×7 or 9×9, whatever you do

Why: banners can fill a rectangle perfectly only if one side is a multiple of 4 (a known tiling theorem).

What the dead pockets look like (share of all dead cells)

Three quarters of dead cells sit in slivers 1 cell wide. A loose 2×2 emblem can only fill the purple kind.

The basics

The fix: the patch rule

The patch rule

An emblem can go down in one of two ways:

Hub: on any empty 2×2. It can trigger and score.
Patch: pinned over any enclosed gap of 1–4 cells. The gap counts as filled.

Patching costs you the emblem’s hub value, so it is a real choice.

Before and after (8×8 board, share of cells left dead)

Random play

Careful (“tidy”) play

Emblems are 25% of the pieces in these runs. With the patch rule, 98% of careful games end with a perfect board. In the docs’ words: dead cells fall from 17% to 2% in random play and to 0% in careful play.

Emblems come in pairs on 8×8

An exact search found a parity lock. On 8×8, only 0, 2, 4 or 6 emblems allow a perfect fill. On 6×6 and 10×10 you need an odd number (1, 3, 5). Players never see this; every layout is checked by the solver.

Mix on 8×8: 10 banners : 6 emblems recommended · 12 : 4 if banners must dominate

The pinwheel

One centre emblem makes 6×6 and 10×10 fillable. The four arms are rectangles that banners fill cleanly. A 10×10 has 1,018 such tilings.

Part 3 · Emblem design A

Rally Point

“Lay your calling cards around emblems. When an emblem is surrounded, it rallies, and every banner touching it scores.”

At a glance
Players
Solo daily, or 2 players
Board
8×8
Pieces
16 tiles: 10 banners + 6 emblems (12 + 4 in solo). Each tile has 1 of 4 suits. In 2-player, your banners carry your colour frame.
How you win
2-player: most points; a tie goes to whoever completed more rallies. Solo: points, +5 for a perfect board, −1 per empty cell.
Play time
16 placements, about 5–7 minutes
Phone fit
Excellent. 48pt cells, 96pt emblems, the 3-tile market under the board
Verdict
★ Prototype next
How a turn works
  1. 1
    Take a tilePick 1 of the 3 face-up tiles.
  2. 2
    Place itBanner: any 4 empty cells in a line. Emblem: a hub on any empty 2×2, or a patch over a gap of 1–4 cells (a patch never rallies).
  3. 3
    Rally!When all 8 cells around a hub are filled (the board edge counts), it fires and every touching banner scores.

How a rally scores

  • Each border cell covered by a banner gives that banner’s owner 1 point, or 2 if the suits match.
  • A long side flush with the emblem covers 2 border cells. An end covers 1, but keeps the far end free to reach a second emblem.
  • 2-player: whoever completes the rally gets +1.
  • At the end, each un-rallied hub scores its current contacts at face value (no suit bonus, no +1).

The game ends when the stack is empty, or when nothing fits after one market refresh. The rally animation is the beat: every touching banner flashes and its points pop on each segment.

Rally Point: a worked example

Two players. P1 banners are blue, P2 banners are orange. The emblem sits at rows 3–4, columns 3–4. Two cells on its right (?) are still open. It is P1’s turn, and P1 holds a banner.

P1 scores 9a: 2 cells × 2 = 4 · new banner: 2 × 2 = 4 · closer +1
P2 scores 6B (suit , no match): 2 × 1 = 2 · C: 2 × 2 = 4
Net +3 for P1

Option 2: half-close

Lay the banner across row 3, columns 5–8. It covers one ? (2 points pending) and opens a lane. But P2 can then close the emblem with any piece in row 4, for +1 and their own 6.

Option 3: wait

Build a second hub elsewhere and hope P2 is forced to close this one later.

Closing an emblem always feeds your opponent too. Who closes, and when, is the heart of the game.

Pinwheel

“Fill the board with banners. Each emblem tells you how many banners touch it and how many point at it.”

At a glance
Players
Solo daily logic puzzle
Board
10×10, one centre goal emblem on weekdays; 3 solver-checked emblems on weekends
Pieces
2–4 givens (greyed-out banners) and a tray of exactly enough banners: 20 or 21
How you win
Every cell filled and every clue exact. The solution is unique. Stars for no hints and under par time.
Play time
3–6 minutes
Phone fit
Good. 10×10 at 39pt cells is the maximum; drag-and-snap with a ghost preview
Verdict
Keep as the daily-puzzle mode
How a turn works
  1. 1
    Read the clueTouch = how many different banners touch the emblem. Point = how many meet it end-on.
  2. 2
    Lay bannersAnywhere on the board. Undo is free.
  3. 3
    Watch for redCells that can no longer be covered glow red at once, so the board teaches itself.

Worked example: clue Touch 7 · Point 5

Solving starts from the clue: “Point 5” means five banners meet the emblem end-on, so most of its border must be banner ends. Test that against the givens. Grey = givens, blue = banners you place.

Banner Front: HQ & Modules

“Part 2’s tile battle (see p. 14), upgraded with emblems.”

At a glance
Players
2 (solo against an AI, or pass-and-play)
Board
8×8 (was 8×7)
Pieces
Your HQ is a 2×2 emblem: your own emblem, centred on your back row. 3 of the 20 deck tiles become 2×2 modules.
How you win
As in Part 2: destroy the enemy HQ, or have more HQ HP after the final battle
Play time
8–10 minutes
Phone fit
As in Part 2. HQ and module icons get 96pt badges, which helps.
Verdict
Write into the Banner Front spec, not a new prototype
How a turn works
  1. 1
    Draw and chooseDraw up to 3 tiles, play 2, discard 1.
  2. 2
    PlaceUnits are banners (4 rotations). A module goes on a 2×2 as a hub, or as a patch over a gap of 1–4 cells.
  3. 3
    BattleOn a Battle order, or when neither a banner nor a module fits anywhere. Initiative 3 → 2 → 1 → 0.

The HQ

It replaces the 1×4 Command banner. 10 HP (the first player has 11), a Blade on all 8 segments, initiative 0. A square HQ reads as “home” at a glance and gives the enemy 8 segments to aim at instead of 10.

Modules (2 HP, no attack)

  • Relay: touching friendly banners get +1 initiative.
  • Armoury: Guns on touching friendly banners deal +1 damage.
  • Bulwark: touching friendly segments ignore Guns.
  • As a patch: 1 HP, keeps its buff.

Worked example: bottom of P1’s side

▲ = Rifles’ Guns on its upper long side. The Armoury A touches both the Rifles and the HQ, so the Rifles fire four 2-damage shots.

35% → 2.8%dead cells when the board-full battle fires: banners only → modules + patch. “Board full” now means actually full.
The enemy’s best answer: a Lancer whose Heavy Gun (2 damage, initiative 3) points at the Armoury. It kills the Armoury before the Rifles fire at initiative 2, so the buff never lands. Modules become the targets players fight over.

Play time

How a move works

    Pick any barOne bar per move.
  2. 2

Collection hook

Your player card: emblem + calling card

In Call of Duty a player’s identity is an emblem plus a calling card. Our game can make that pair the centre of collecting.

Why emblems make collecting more exciting

  1. A second axis. “I have this card but not its emblem” is a gap that is easy to read, and gaps drive collecting.
  2. Pairings turn two collections into combos. Each new unlock can complete something you already own.
  3. The emblem is the hero of every rally. The 96pt badge gets the big animation, so a new emblem is seen.

Pairings Objective only

Original emblem and banner pairs made for each other. When a paired banner touches its emblem as it rallies, a unique animation plays and a stamp goes into your Pairing Book (silhouettes with unlock hints).

Emblem orders (sets) Cosmetic

Emblems come in themed orders of 4, each tied to a banner series. Complete both to unlock a board theme and frame.

“Order of the Wolf: 2 of 4 emblems, 3 of 5 banners”

Emblem ranks Cosmetic

An emblem gains a star each time it rallies or locks, 1 to 5 stars. “Rally with 20 different emblems” rewards breadth.

Daily prize emblem Cosmetic

Solve the day’s Pinwheel to earn its goal emblem: a chase earned in this game.

Rally mosaic Cosmetic

A finished Rally Point board is a mosaic of your banners around your emblems, shareable as an image.

Call of Duty loop Hypothetical link

Unlock an emblem in CoD → it becomes a hub face here → own the matching calling card too, and its Pairing slot lights up.

Fair play: an emblem’s gameplay (suit, module type) comes from a balanced neutral table hashed from its ID. Ranked play uses neutral sets; your collection only skins them. Until there is an agreement, prototypes use only original art and a mock unlock feed.
