Recommendation: prototype two

  1. Point Man, the flagship. A 1v1 Hardpoint duel on a 3×3 room map, and your deck is generated from your loadout: weapons give Fire cards, attachments edit those cards, perks are passive slots and killstreaks sit in a tray. This makes the gunsmith-and-unlock loop the deckbuilding itself, so “I unlocked a foregrip” directly means “my deck changed”.
  2. Snapshot, the cheap second. A 4-minute simultaneous-reveal Search and Destroy duel (“who pre-aimed the angle?”). It is the purest translation of fast time-to-kill and reads, it is phone-portrait native, and it shares Point Man’s weapon table and loadout data. It could ship later as Point Man’s quick mode.

Four decisions are needed from the studio head (§11): which concepts to prototype, who the first opponent is, how much is hidden on the map, and which screen to design first. Recommended answers are marked.

Section 1

What makes a CoD match feel like CoD

1.1 Seven design pillars

1One body, many lives.

You are one operator. Your body is your only piece, so every risk is personal. Death is brief (you are back in seconds), so you play boldly rather than freezing.

Card testthe player never controls more than one figure, and dying costs tempo, never the match.
2First to see, first to shoot.

Time-to-kill is short. The fight is decided before it starts, by who held the angle, who had the better range and who acted first.

Card testmost exchanges resolve in one or two shots (Health 4, damage 1 to 4). There are no long health-bar slugfests.
3Position is power.

Sightlines, cover, power positions, flanks and spawn logic matter. Where you stand changes what your gun is worth: an SMG in a room versus a sniper down a lane.

Card testthe same card is good or bad depending on the map square you play it from.
4The loadout is your identity.

Before the match you make a personal, expressive build (primary, secondary, equipment, perks, field upgrade, streaks), and it shapes how you want to play.

Card testtwo players with the same rank and different loadouts face different decisions every turn.
5The streak is the tension.

Kills without dying escalate your power. Each life asks you to play safe and protect the streak, or push for the next reward.

Card testa visible streak meter, three chosen rewards, and a reset on death.
6The objective drags you out of hiding.

Score-to-win modes (Team Deathmatch, Domination, Hardpoint, Search and Destroy) punish camping and force fights in known places.

Card testthe score comes mainly from map spaces, so standing still loses.
7Every match moves a bar.

Weapon XP, attachment unlocks, camo challenges and calling cards mean even a loss feels like progress, and “one more match” is always close.

Card testevery match awards XP to the weapon you used and progress on at least one visible challenge.
+Loud and readable feedback.

Eighth, flavour pillar, applied everywhere rather than designed. Hit markers, a kill feed and a streak call-in moment. KillingCard already has these as FX (§10).

1.2 What translates poorly to cards, and how to fake it

Doesn’t translateWhyHow we fake it
Aim and reflexesCards have no aim skill.Replace aim with reads and initiative: hidden “Hold Angle” reactions (Point Man), simultaneous reveal with a stance triangle (Snapshot), or a time track where slow guns pay ticks (Tick Rate). Skill becomes predicting the opponent, the card-game equivalent of pre-aiming.
Real time / simultaneityAlternating turns let the defender react, which turns a 0.3-second fight into a polite exchange.Use reaction windows (overwatch), simultaneous reveal, or a time track. Never use “I shoot, you shoot back, I shoot”.
Fast time-to-kill vs card-game health poolsCard games like 20–30 HP.Health 4 and damage 1–4. Use cover and plates as the only buffers.
Fog of war, footsteps, minimapA board is fully visible.Hidden position cards, “Noise” (sprinting reveals you), and recon killstreaks that reveal hidden information. Concepts differ on how much is hidden (Q3).
6v6 chaos and respawn churnToo many bodies to track on a phone.Use 1v1 or 3v3 at most. Respawn becomes a fresh hand plus a tempo penalty (the respawn timer).
Fluid movement (slide, mantle, jump)Grids are clunky.Use small node or room graphs. Movement cards (Slide, Mantle, Sprint) carry the verbs as small bonuses.
Recoil, attachments as feelAttachments are about feel, not numbers.Each attachment is a trade-off on a card: +1 Long but Handling +1, or +2 Fire cards but −1 Close. The player feels the gunsmith as deck texture.
Spawn trapping, campingThese degenerate into stalemates.Rotating objectives (Hardpoint), spawns placed far from the enemy, and grenades that ignore cover.

Section 3 · Concept 1

Point Man

1v1 Hardpoint duel; your deck is your loadout
Players1v1
ModeHardpoint
WinFirst to 8 points
MatchAbout 10–12 min
Best screenPhone portrait: good

Pitch. Two operators fight over a rotating hardpoint on a 3×3 grid of rooms. Before the match you build a loadout, and the loadout generates your 24-card deck. Your primary contributes its Fire cards, your attachments edit them (more cards, better range, cheaper to fire), equipment adds a few utility cards, perks sit in three passive slots, and killstreaks wait in a tray until your streak points earn them. When you go down you lose your hand and respawn with a fresh one, so respawning feels like coming back in with a new plan. The skill is in positioning (which room, which sightline) and in Hold Angle: a face-down Fire card that shoots first when the enemy walks into your line. That is the “they pre-aimed me” moment.

The map

9 rooms (A1–C3) with walls, doorways, windows (Mantle only) and some roofed rooms. Range: same room = Close; an adjacent room through an opening = Mid; two rooms away along a straight line of aligned openings (a printed sightline) = Long. Rooms have Cover 0 or 1. Each side has 2 spawn rooms in opposite corners. You respawn in the one farther from the enemy. Maps are hand-made (target 4 at launch). Positions are public; Hold Angles are hidden.

How a turn works

1Draw & gain T
Draw up to 5. Gain 3 T (2 T on the turn you respawn).
2Spend T
Spend T on any mix of: Step (1 T: move 1 room through an opening), movement cards, Fire (cost = Handling; damage by range, minus cover), equipment (1 T), killstreaks from the tray (0 T), and the field upgrade (0 T when charged).
3Hold Angle (1 T)
Put any Fire card face-down on one sightline or doorway from your room. Until your next turn, the first time the enemy moves into, or fires along, that line, you immediately resolve that Fire first, before their action. Then the card is discarded.
4End of turn
If you are on the hardpoint and the enemy is not, +1 point and +1 streak point.
5Downed
You lose your hand and your streak points. You respawn at the start of your next turn (that is the respawn penalty). The enemy gets +2 streak points.

Win

The first to 8 points wins. The hardpoint moves every 3 rounds (B2, then A3, then C1, then B2…). There is a cap of 14 rounds; at the cap the leader wins, and a tie goes to sudden death on B2. Match length: about 10–12 minutes (one turn is about 25–40 seconds).

Platform fit

Phone portrait: good. The 3×3 map sits on the top half, the hand of 5 at the bottom, with the perk and streak strips between them. PC and landscape: map on the left, hand and loadout on the right. Async PvP is possible because turns are discrete.

Meta progression

Each weapon has its own XP (gained per Fire card played, and doubled on kills) and levels 1–20 that unlock attachments. That is the core pull, because each unlock changes the deck. Operator level unlocks perks, equipment and killstreaks. Camos come from per-weapon challenges (“10 Long downs with the Longreach”) and change the card art finish only. Calling cards are earned for feats (a 3-kill streak, a perfect hill) and appear as your banner on the match intro. Cosmetics never change gameplay.

Section 4 · Concept 2

Fireteam

3v3 Domination, one operator per player
Players3v3 (humans or bots)
ModeDomination
WinFirst to 12 points
MatchAbout 8–10 min
Best screenLandscape-first

Pitch. Two fireteams of three. Every player (a human, or a bot ally with a personality) runs exactly one operator. The map is 3 lanes × 5 depth bands, with a Domination flag in the middle of each lane. Each round all six operators plan in secret: where to move, and which kit tile to use. The plans are revealed together, then each lane’s firefight resolves in a readable order (KillingCard’s beat system). Team roles come from loadouts: the SMG entry player, the LMG anchor, the sniper on the long lane. Solo play is you plus 2 bot allies against 3 bots. Co-op is 3 humans against AI. Full 3v3 PvP comes later.

The map

Lanes Left / Mid / Right. Bands from your side: Spawn, Side, Flag, their Side, their Spawn. Range: same band = Close, 1 band apart = Mid, 2 bands apart = Long, and you can only shoot within your own lane. Your own Side band gives you Cover 1. The Flag band has Cover 0. Each map card (there are 5) changes 1–2 cells: “Mid Flag has Cover 1”, “Right lane Long sightline blocked”.

Loadout becomes a kit board (no deck)

Each operator has 4 kit tiles: two universal and two set by the primary. A used tile is on cooldown for one round. Lethal and tactical have 1 charge per life. Perks are passive. The field upgrade charges over 3 rounds. Killstreaks are per operator (2 / 3 / 5 kills without dying), and their effects help the team (a Recon Drone reveals the enemy’s planned lanes before your next lock-in).

How a round works

1Plan (secret, 30 s online)
Each operator chooses a move (stay, advance or fall back 1 band, or shift lane within your Side or Spawn band) and at most one kit tile or equipment. Humans can ping lanes to bots.
2Reveal
Movement resolves, then smoke and flashes.
3Firefights, lane by lane, Left to Right
Within a lane, the highest Initiative shoots first (Pistol 5, SMG/Shotgun 4, AR 3, LMG 2, Sniper 1, but Sniper is 5 at Long). Each operator fires once at the nearest enemy in range. A downed operator doesn’t fire.
4Flags
A lane’s flag flips to a team that has operators in the Flag band while the other team has none.
5Score
+1 per flag you hold. Downed operators respawn at their Spawn band next round, in any lane.

Win

The first team to 12 points, with a cap of 12 rounds. Match length: about 8–10 minutes.

Meta progression

As in Point Man (weapon XP, attachments that upgrade kit tiles, camos, calling cards), plus bot ally unlocks with personalities (KillingCard’s AiPersonality weights).

Platform fit

Landscape-first (phone landscape, tablet, PC): three lanes side by side is KillingCard’s column board. Phone portrait is cramped (6 operators plus flags), but it works if the lanes stack vertically.

Section 5 · Concept 3

Behind the Line

Roguelite single-operator campaign (PvE)
PlayersSolo, offline
ModeThree-act campaign run
WinBeat the act-3 boss
LengthRun 25–35 min · fight 2–3
Best screenPhone portrait: excellent

Pitch. A solo run. You take one operator through a three-act campaign map of missions, safehouses, armories and intel events, ending in a commander boss. Combat is a card-crawl: each encounter is a short line of enemy cards that telegraph their intent (“aiming at you: 2 damage next turn”), and you choose between three positions (Left cover, Centre, Right flank) that change who can see you. Your deck is a classic deckbuilder of actions. Your weapon levels up mid-run, and each level offers 3 attachments. Clean streaks (kills without taking Health damage) earn killstreaks inside a mission. This is where the “one more run” collection loop lives.

The map: two layers

The run map is KillingCard Run v2’s map unchanged in structure: 9 steps, 3 paths, forks into Armory (shop, currency REQ), Intel (events), Safehouse (rest), Elite (HVT) and Boss. The encounter is a 3-room mission (two fights and an exfil fight). Each fight has your 3 positions against 1–4 enemy cards at Close, Mid or Long, some with cover. An enemy has a sightline to you unless your position is behind cover from its side (Left cover blocks enemies on the right flank, and so on).

An encounter: enemy intent and your three positions

Loadout → cards and slots

You start with Primary + Secondary on the board (their stats from §2) and a 10-card starter deck. Perks are relics found at elites and Armories (3 slots; you may swap). The Field Upgrade is a per-mission charge. Killstreaks: choose 3 at the run start, earned by clean-streak points within a mission. Weapon XP (from kills) levels the gun: each level offers 3 attachments, pick 1, maximum 5. A second primary found mid-run can be swapped in, and your Fire cards adopt the new gun’s stats, which is exactly CoD’s “pick up a weapon”.

Win

Beat the act-3 commander boss (each boss has one unique rule, like KillingCard Run v2’s bosses). A run takes about 25–35 minutes; a single fight takes 2–3 minutes.

Meta progression

The strongest of the six. Runs unlock new weapons, attachments, perks and killstreaks into the pools. There are operators with a passive and a once-per-run ability (Run v2’s Operators), camos from challenges, calling cards for boss kills and modifiers, and Threat levels (Hardcore and so on).

Platform fit

Phone portrait: excellent (Slay the Spire-like layout: enemy line on top, you in the middle, hand at the bottom). PC is fine. Offline.

Section 6 · Concept 4

Tick Rate

Draft your loadout, then fight on a time track
Players1v1 arena
ModeDraft, then Team Deathmatch
WinFirst to 5 downs
MatchAbout 10 min incl. draft
Best screenPC / landscape

Pitch. An arena 1v1 in two acts. Draft (3 minutes): both players snake-draft from three open packs of 4 loadout pieces (weapons, attachments, perks, equipment, streaks). Taking the sniper so your opponent can’t is a real strategy. Fight (6–8 minutes): a Team Deathmatch to 5 downs on a 7-node map, with no turns. Both operators sit on a shared time track: whoever is further behind in time acts next, and every action costs ticks. A sniper’s aim costs 3 ticks and an SMG’s costs 1. That is how real time and time-to-kill are faked: a heavy gun is literally slow. An aimed operator gets a free reaction shot when the enemy walks into their line.

The map

7 nodes (2 spawns, Alley, Market, Courtyard [Cover 1], Tower [high ground], Underpass) joined by move edges. Sightline edges are printed separately and tagged C, M or L. Positions are public.

Loadout → no deck; an action bar with costs (in ticks)

Everyone has the same actions, priced by the loadout.

ActionSMGARShotgunLMGSniper

Swap to pistol costs 1 (Aim 0, Fire 1). Equipment costs 2. Attachments shift costs (Quick Stock: Aim −1; Long Barrel: +1 Long, Aim +1). Perks bend rules (Soft Step: Step −1; Long Breath: staying Aimed has no upkeep). Reaction: when an enemy moves into, or along, your aimed sightline, you fire immediately and pay the Fire cost afterwards. Stepping breaks your aim.

How the fight works

1Lowest time acts
The operator with the lower time acts (ties: the one who acted less recently). They take one action and advance their marker by its cost.
2Reactions
Reactions interrupt at once.
3Downed
You respawn at your far spawn at your current time + 6 ticks (the respawn timer), and your streak resets. Killstreaks come at 2 / 3 / 4 downs without dying (Recon Drone: the enemy’s aim lines are shown for 6 ticks; Sentry Turret: a permanent 1-damage reaction shot on one sightline; Mortar Barrage: 3 damage on a node after a 4-tick delay, so it can be dodged).

Win

The first to 5 downs. A cap of 120 ticks; at the cap the leader wins. Match: about 10 minutes including the draft.

Meta progression

Ranked draft ladders. Your collection decides the draft pool’s cosmetics (camos and calling cards), never the power. Weekly “pack themes” (e.g. a shotgun week).

Platform fit

PC and landscape are best, since the time track reads well as a horizontal bar. Phone portrait works: the map on top, a 5-button action bar, the track as a thin strip. The rules are deterministic, so it is great for replays and tournaments.

Sightline

Map-control board game with sightline tiles
Players1v1 or 2v2
ModeDomination, 3 flags
WinFirst to 15 points
Match20–30 min (1v1) · 30–40 (2v2)
Best screenPC & tablet landscape

Pitch. The deepest and most “board game” of the six. A 1v1 or 2v2 Domination match on a modular map built from 9 square tiles (each 3×3 cells) that the players draft and place before the match. Each tile has printed walls, cover crates and sightline lanes, so building the map is the first tactic: “I’ll give my sniper a long lane to B.” Each player has a personal deck plus the loadout board, and earns in-match XP from kills and flags to buy attachments and equipment from a shared Armory market. That is the “level up during the match” feeling. It is designed so it can also be printed and played on a table (CardZone and Warbuilder lineage).

The map

A 9×9 grid. LOS runs only along printed rows, columns and diagonals and is blocked by walls. Range: 1–2 cells = Close, 3–5 = Mid, 6+ = Long. Crates give Cover 1. There are three flags (A, B, C) on the centre tiles.

Loadout → board plus deck

Section 8 · Concept 6

Snapshot

Simultaneous-reveal Search and Destroy duel
Players1v1
ModeSearch and Destroy
WinFirst to 4 rounds
MatchAbout 4–6 min
Best screenPhone portrait: excellent

Pitch. It looks like a 4-minute party game and has a ladder underneath. Each round is one life: the attacker has to plant at a site, and the defender has to stop them. Each beat of the round, both players secretly choose a Position (where you’ll be) and a Stance (Hold, Peek, Rush, Utility, or Plant/Defuse), then reveal. If the two positions share a sightline, it’s a fight: Hold beats Rush (they ran into your crosshair), Rush beats Peek (you closed before they settled), Peek beats Hold (the shoulder-peek baited the held angle). The winner shoots with their weapon’s damage at that range. The same stance means both fire at once, which can trade. With no contact, nothing is revealed except Noise from rushing. It is the purest version of “who pre-aimed the angle?”

The map

Six positions in a graph: A Site, A Ramp, Mid, Connector, B Short, B Site, plus the attacker’s spawn (which links to A Ramp, Mid and B Short). Each sightline edge is tagged with a range: A Ramp–A Site M, Mid–A Site L, Mid–Connector C, Connector–B Site M, B Short–B Site C, Mid–B Short L. Sharing a position = Close. You move one edge per beat (two with Rush). The launch target is 4 maps.

Loadout → no deck; an always-available hand

Your hand is the map’s position cards plus 5 stance cards, every beat. The loadout adds to them: the weapon sets damage by range; the Secondary gives a 1-damage “Sidearm Peek”.

How a round works

1Health
HP 4 (wounds carry over within the round; a fresh 4 each round).
2Beat (up to 5 per round)
Choose Position + Stance face-down, then reveal. A fight happens if the positions share a sightline or are the same. Resolve the stance triangle; the winner deals weapon damage at that range minus cover (sites have Cover 1 for the defender). Equal stances: both deal damage. No contact: positions stay hidden, and a Rush reveals the rusher’s position afterwards (Noise).
3Plant / Defuse
Plant: the attacker uses the Plant stance at a site with no contact that beat. Defuse: the defender then has 2 beats to use Defuse at that site with no contact (being hit cancels it).
4Round end
A down (an elimination), a detonation (the attacker wins), a defuse, or 5 beats with no plant (the defender wins).

Win

The first to 4 rounds, swapping sides after 3. Match: about 4–6 minutes (one beat is about 8 seconds).

Meta progression

Ranked tiers with seasonal calling cards, weapon XP and camos, map unlocks, and “clutch” challenges (win a round below 2 HP). It shares Point Man’s loadout collection if both ship.

Platform fit

Phone portrait: excellent. The map graph on top, two short card rows (positions and stances) at the bottom, two taps per beat. It works for PC and async (“play a beat a day”).

9.2 Recommendation: prototype Point Man first and Snapshot second

Point Man is the one that answers the head’s brief most directly: one character, two weapons, perks and stuff, improving our own character, fighting on a map.

1 · Point Man

  • It is the only concept where the collection loop and the match loop are the same object. Unlocking an attachment literally rewrites your deck, which makes pillar 7 (“every match moves a bar”) felt in the next match, not just seen in a menu. That is the strongest retention hook of the six.
  • Hold Angle gives cards an honest skill expression in place of aim, and the 3×3 room map is the smallest board that still has sightlines, flanks and power positions. It fits a phone screen in portrait.
  • It uses all four CoD loadout layers (weapons, equipment, perks, streaks) without any of them being decorative.
  • The risk is balance: every loadout generates a different deck. It is manageable, because the shared weapon table (§2) has only 6 rows and attachments are small trade-offs. A headless sim (KillingCard-style tests/tools/*_check.gd) can sweep loadout pairs.

2 · Snapshot, the cheap second

  • It can be paper-tested in a day and prototyped in about a week. It isolates the riskiest feel question, “does a card read feel like a gunfight?”, at the lowest cost.
  • It shares Point Man’s weapon table, loadout data and collection, so if Point Man ships, Snapshot becomes its quick mode (and a daily ladder), not a separate game.
  • Its risk (“it’s rock-paper-scissors”) is tested directly: if weapon range, map positions and Noise make the reads informed rather than random, the stance triangle works. If playtesters call it a coin-flip, we learn that cheaply.
